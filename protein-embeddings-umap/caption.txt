🧬 From protein to point cloud: what an embedding is, why people run PCA or UMAP on it, and what that can and cannot tell you. One real paper, one real protein.

Swipe through 👉

1️⃣ 1,280 numbers: a protein language model turns every residue into a list of numbers it worked out during training. That list is the embedding. Nothing in it is named; what matters is that similar residues get similar lists.
2️⃣ Context: the model reads the whole chain, so one mutation nudges every residue's vector a little. The mutated one moves most. That is the vector the paper keeps.
3️⃣ PCA: rotate the cloud and keep the two widest axes. Honest distances, no randomness, faint clusters.
4️⃣ UMAP: find who is near whom in all 1,280 dimensions, then lay it out in two so neighbours stay neighbours. Clusters pop, gaps between clusters mean little.
5️⃣ Distance as a score: "the further the vector moved, the worse the variant" is tempting. On transthyretin it is a coin flip, ROC-AUC 0.51.
6️⃣ Seeds: four random starts, four different maps. Neighbourhoods survive, shapes do not. The paper repeats UMAP ten times for that reason.
7️⃣ The paper's result: a classifier on the raw 1,280 numbers won (ROC-AUC 0.79) and was best calibrated. Squashing with UMAP first did not help (0.59). Use UMAP to look, the full vector to predict.

Data: ESM2-650M embeddings of 134 ClinVar missense variants in transthyretin, shared by the authors of Lomoio et al., Bioinformatics Advances 2026 (MIT licence). No model was run for this post; every plot comes from their vectors. Code and vectors on GitHub: link in bio 🐐
