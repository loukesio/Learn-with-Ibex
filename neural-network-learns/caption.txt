🧠 How a neural network learns, on a real-looking job: is this DNA variant disease-causing? Four facts about the variant go in, one probability comes out. Weights, loss, epochs, learning rate, overfitting, one tiny network trained live. Two video slides: the weights changing (slide 2) and the decision boundary bending (slide 6).

Swipe through 👉

1️⃣ The network, on video: 4 inputs (how common the variant is, how conserved the position, how much it damages the protein, how close to a splice site), 8 hidden neurons, 1 output. Every line is a weight. 49 numbers in total, and you can watch them change over 60 epochs.
2️⃣ Forward pass: data in, prediction out. With random weights the network already answers, just badly: 52%, a coin flip.
3️⃣ Loss and backprop: the loss is one number for how wrong. Backpropagation finds, for each weight, which direction lowers it, and every weight takes a small step. That is one iteration. An epoch is one pass over all 200 variants, 10 iterations here.
4️⃣ Learning rate: the size of each step. 0.02 crawls, 0.5 is right, 5 bounces around.
5️⃣ Epochs, on video: the network discovers that rare and conserved means dangerous, the boundary bends into place, the loss falls.
6️⃣ Overfitting: 40 variants, 32 neurons, 600 epochs. Training loss keeps falling, validation loss turns up after epoch 25. Early stopping keeps the weights from the best epoch.
7️⃣ Evaluate: 100 variants the network never saw, touched once at the end. 92%, 8 wrong, 5 of them false alarms. From here the Evaluation Metrics series takes over.

The variants are simulated with a rule that mimics real genetics, so the right answers are known and the network has to rediscover the rule. The whole network is 70 lines of base R, no packages. Code on GitHub: link in bio 🐐
